Aluminum vs UPVC: the complete comparison, and which suits your home

We hear this question every day at our showroom. The short answer: both are excellent, but each has its place. This guide explains the difference clearly so you can choose with confidence.

The answer in two linesChoose UPVC if your priority is insulation against heat, noise and dust (bedrooms, flats on main roads, high floors). Choose aluminum if you need large openings and big glass in a slim frame (balconies, facades, shops), or the best balance of price and durability.

What are aluminum and UPVC?

Aluminum windows are made from aluminum-alloy profiles: light, strong, rust-free, and powder-coated in many colors. Their strength allows slim frames that carry large glass.

UPVC is unplasticized, rigid PVC extruded into profiles with internal air chambers and a steel reinforcing core. Those chambers are the secret behind its high heat and sound insulation.

Insulation: heat, noise and dust

If your flat faces a main road or sits on a high floor, insulation is the most important criterion. UPVC profiles have multiple air chambers that block heat and sound transfer, and with tight gaskets and casement or tilt-and-turn opening, the window seals very tightly.

Aluminum conducts heat by nature, but with double glazing, good gaskets and brush seals it reaches very good insulation, especially in hinged windows.

Showroom tip: sliding windows — in any material — insulate less than casement windows because the sashes run on a track. If insulation is your priority, choose casement or tilt-and-turn.

Looks and large openings

This is where aluminum wins. Its strength allows slim profiles to carry wide glass panels without bending, giving more daylight and a cleaner look — which is why it is the natural choice for facades, balconies and shopfronts.

UPVC looks very elegant in standard sizes and works well for arched shapes and classic grids, as you can see in our gallery.

What drives the price?

Prices move with material costs, so instead of a fixed number that quickly goes out of date, here are the factors that actually determine what a window costs:

  • Profile type and depth — number of chambers in UPVC, profile weight in aluminum.
  • Glass — single or double, clear, frosted or tempered.
  • Opening style — casement and tilt-and-turn usually cost more than sliding.
  • Size and shape — arches and grids take more work.
  • Hardware — locks, handles, insect screens and hardware brand.

For an accurate price for your home, send us your measurements or book a site visit and we will prepare a detailed quote.

So which should I choose?

Choose UPVC if:

  • Your flat faces a busy road or another noise source.
  • You want less heat and dust and lower air-conditioning bills.
  • You are replacing bedroom and living-room windows or bathroom doors.

Choose aluminum if:

  • You have wide openings or a balcony that needs large glass.
  • You are building a shop, showroom or office front.
  • You want a slim frame in a distinctive color, or the best price-to-durability ratio.

Many homes combine both: UPVC for bedrooms and aluminum for balconies. The same logic applies to kitchens: aluminum kitchens for humid spaces and wood kitchens for richer finishes.

FAQ

Aluminum vs UPVC — FAQ

Can’t find your question? Send it on WhatsApp and we will reply quickly.

Is UPVC better than aluminum?

Not absolutely. UPVC is better for heat and sound insulation; aluminum is better for large openings, slim frames and colors. The right choice depends on the location and your needs.

Which is cheaper, aluminum or UPVC?

Price depends more on the profile, glass, opening style and size than on the material itself. Send us your measurements and we will quote both so you can compare.

Can UPVC handle Egyptian summer heat?

Yes. Quality profiles are UV-stabilized and designed for high temperatures without warping or changing color.

Can I combine aluminum and UPVC in the same flat?

Yes, and it is very common: UPVC for bedrooms and living rooms for insulation, aluminum for balconies and large openings.
